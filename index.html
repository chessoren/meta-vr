The problem

Re-reading feels like studying. It mostly isn’t.

It’s what nearly everyone does the night before an exam: highlight, re-read, re-read again. It feels productive because the page starts to look familiar. But familiar isn’t the same as remembered — and the exam asks you to retrieve, not to recognise.

In a large 2013 review of ten popular study techniques, psychologists rated re-reading and highlighting low utility. The two that earned the top rating — testing yourself and spacing your reviews over time — are exactly the ones students skip, because they’re effortful and hard to schedule.

  1. 1885Hermann Ebbinghaus charts the forgetting curve: what we learn fades fast unless we come back to it.
  2. 2006Roediger & Karpicke: students who tested themselves remembered more a week later than students who re-studied.
  3. 2013Dunlosky et al.: practice testing and distributed practice rated highest; re-reading, low.

The method of loci

A 2,500‑year‑old technique. Finally effortless.

Around 500 BC, the poet Simonides of Ceos stepped out of a banquet hall moments before its roof collapsed. The guests were beyond recognition — yet Simonides could name every one of them, by remembering where each had been sitting. That is the memory palace: bind each thing you want to remember to a place you know by heart, then walk the route in your mind.

It is still the core technique of memory champions. In a brain-imaging study of world-class memorisers, nine out of ten said they used it. And ordinary people who practised it for six weeks more than doubled how many words they could recall — with much of the gain still there four months later.

So why doesn’t everyone use it? Because inventing a vivid image for every fact and memorising a route for every chapter is exhausting. Loci does that part for you.

    Each notion arrives in your hands as a small, absurd, moving scene. Canberra? A kangaroo bouncing on your lamp. Pinch it, carry it, set it down on the object you choose. Your furniture becomes a route, left to right from your seat — the same route, every night.

Why it works

Three levers, pulled at once.

Most study apps pick one. Loci refuses to trade any of them away.

01

Real spatial anchoring

The fact sits on your lamp — not in a virtual room you’ll never see again. In the exam hall, you close your eyes and walk your own bedroom.

02

Vivid mnemonic scenes

Bizarre, funny, exaggerated, moving — the rules mnemonists have taught for centuries. Avogadro’s 6.02 × 10²³ becomes a giant avocado juggling six balls.

03

Active spaced recall

You never just re-read. You retrieve each answer at the moment you’re about to forget it. Miss one and its scene replays — even more exaggerated.

Built for hands & glasses

Your hands are the controller. Your chair is the cockpit.

Every interaction is seated, within 60 cm, and readable in the narrow central field of view of Meta VR Glasses. Nothing to hold, nothing to learn twice.

  • Gaze + pinchSelect an object. No outstretched arm — fine in an airplane seat.
  • Pinch, move, releasePlace a scene. The natural way to put something down.
  • Voice or bubbleAnswer aloud — or pinch one of three bubbles. Hands-free when you want it.
  • Turn palm upReveal the answer, then grade yourself. Readable, impossible by accident.

The AI does the tedious part. Then it steps aside.

Composed, never generated

Loci never generates 3D on the fly. It assembles scenes from its own library of low-poly models and animations, following the mnemonist’s rules: exaggerated, moving, absurd, tied to a sound or a meaning. So a scene is ready in seconds, runs smoothly in the headset, and always looks like it belongs in your room.

Unplug it. Loci still works.

Questions

Asked by the curious and the sceptical.

Which headsets does Loci run on?

Loci is a WebXR app for Meta Quest Browser. It is built and tested on Meta Quest 2 and designed for Quest 3 and 3S, whose depth sensing understands your room even better. Everything essential sits in the central field of view, so it is ready for Meta VR Glasses too.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Open the link in Meta Quest Browser and enter. You can add it to your library as an app for one-tap launch. Your phone just needs a browser to import a course.

What if my room isn’t detected?

Loci uses the furniture from your headset’s Space Setup — desk, bed, shelf, lamp, window. If your room is sparse or unknown, the flame helps you pin anchors on your walls by hand, and those work just as well.

Will my scenes still be there tomorrow?

Yes. Each scene is stored relative to its piece of furniture, and Loci re-aligns your room every session from the furniture it recognises. Take the headset off mid-session and you’ll come back to the exact same moment.

Can I import a course in French?

Yes. The interface is in English, but courses can be in English or French — accents render correctly and answers are matched without being fussy about them. Voice answers are English for now; the three bubbles always work.

What happens to my notes and my room?

There are no accounts. Your room layout, your progress and your voice stay on the headset. The course you import is sent to our server — and, when AI is on, to Anthropic’s Claude API — only to be split into notions and handed to your headset through a pairing code that expires within the hour.

Is this proven to work?

The ingredients are among the best-studied in memory research: the method of loci, retrieval practice and spaced repetition. Loci itself is new, so we’re measuring it honestly — a small study with classmates comparing Loci to re-reading, with recall tested after 48 hours and after a week. We’ll publish what we find, whatever it is.

How long is a session?

The first one takes about five minutes and ends with you recalling five capitals from memory. After that, a daily session is five to ten minutes: only the notions that are due will glow.
